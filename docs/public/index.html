Features

A small app with a serious privacy guarantee.

Everything you expect from a notes app, built on a core that keeps the server in the dark.

End-to-end encryption

Notes are sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 on your device. The relay only ever holds ciphertext.

Live shared lists

Send a link. The first person to open it joins. Edits merge instantly, online or off.

No account needed

Start writing immediately. A 12-word phrase is all it takes to bring a second device along.

Offline-first

Every tap works without a network. Changes wait in a local log and sync when you’re back.

Labels & search

Reminders

Scheduled on your device, so the server never learns when or what they’re about.

Photos & audio

Attach images and sound files. Each is encrypted in chunks under a key of its own.

Security

Private is the architecture, not a setting.

Every note gets its own key. Updates are encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 and signed by your device before they leave. The relay stores and forwards them — it holds no keys and can’t read a word.

  • Per-note keys, wrapped by a master key in your OS keychain
  • Signatures verified before anything is decrypted
  • A 12-word phrase brings a second device along
  • Self-host the relay; no EasyNotes account required

EasyNotes has not yet had an independent security audit. The design notes say so plainly — read them before trusting it with anything that matters.

Own your relay

Run it on your own box.

The relay is a small Rust service over Postgres. Put it on a NAS, a VPS, or the machine under your desk. Notes are still encrypted before they reach it, so self-hosting is about control, not trust.

  • AGPL-3.0 source, client and server
  • Docker image and a Synology setup guide
  • Quotas, rate limits and Prometheus metrics built in

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Is it really end-to-end encrypted? +
Yes. Note contents and attachments are encrypted on your device before they’re sent. The relay stores and forwards ciphertext; it never receives the keys.
Do I need an account? +
No. You can start writing with no account at all. A 12-word recovery phrase is optional, and it’s what brings a second device to the same notes.
What if I lose my recovery phrase? +
Nobody can recover it for you — that’s the point, and the cost. Without the phrase, a new device can’t open the account document that holds your note keys. Keep it somewhere safe.
How does sharing work without email? +
Sharing makes a link that carries one secret. The first person to open it joins under their own name. The link works once, for one account, and expires after seven days.
Can I use it offline? +
Yes. Every action works without a network. Edits wait in a local log and sync when a connection returns; the app quietly tells you which state you’re in.
Can I self-host it? +
Yes. The relay is open source, runs on Postgres, and ships with a Synology setup guide. You choose a relay in the app — EasyNotes doesn’t pick one for you.
Has it been audited? +
Not yet. An external review is scheduled before 1.0, and the design docs say so plainly. Until then, treat it as promising rather than proven.
What does it cost? +
The app is open source and free to self-host. A hosted option is still being worked out; there’s no subscription today.
